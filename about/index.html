About dicotic

I think of everything as a story.

Attention is not linear. It is layered, shifting, and often capable of holding more than we tend to assume at once.

dicotic began from a simple tension:

two streams. one mind.

Origin

In 2023, while running through the hills of Berkeley, I found myself frustrated by a simple constraint. I had to choose between listening to inspiring music or listening to important audiobooks.

Music carried emotion and momentum. Audiobooks carried ideas that mattered. But they existed in separate moments, competing for the same attention.

I started experimenting with a different approach. An old iPod nano, two sets of wireless earbuds, and a simple split: music in one ear, audiobook in the other.

What began as an experiment quickly became a way of moving through everyday life. Running, thinking, working—listening stopped being singular.

The idea stayed with me long after the runs ended.

Building

Over time, it became clear that this wasn’t just a personal workaround. It was something others might also find useful.

With a little help from my children — Sivan, Matan, and Aviva — I began building dicotic as an app and eventually released it on March 20, 2026.

What started as a small experiment in listening became a way of exploring how attention can be held differently.

What it is

dicotic lets you listen to two streams at the same time — music, podcasts, audiobooks, lectures, language learning — layered together in a way that feels natural rather than fragmented.

Not to divide attention, but to let it overlap without losing coherence.

Continuity

dicotic is intentionally small.

It is built slowly, independently, and without a larger system behind it.

It continues through the people who find it useful enough to keep it alive.

If dicotic has helped you experience listening differently, you can support its continued development.
